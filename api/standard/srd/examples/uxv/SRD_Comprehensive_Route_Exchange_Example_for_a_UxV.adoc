== A Comprehensive Route Exchange Example for a UxV

=== Purpose

This document shows what an OGC API -- Routes *route response* looks like for an uncrewed vehicle when the route carries *actions*. A route response is a *feature collection*: the `Route` class of the diagram is the collection, its «property» attributes are members of the collection, and its features are the four «FeatureType» classes `RouteOverview`, `Start`, `RouteSegment` and `Destination`. The collection embeds the `RouteDefinition` that describes it (composition, role `describedBy`).

*Input and output are different things.* The route definition is the *input* to the route server: waypoints, a mode, and spatial and temporal constraints. It has no actions, because waypoints are inputs to the algorithms. The route is the *output*: the server creates the geometry, adds the points it needs, allocates the vehicle, and *creates all the actions*.

The example is a multirotor UAV that inspects two towers, drops a sensor with a winch and returns. It uses 21 different commands of the Common Command Registry for Uncrewed Systems on four targets (vehicle, camera, gimbal, winch), with 4 route-level actions, 15 waypoint actions and 4 segment actions. Coordinates and identifiers are made up, and the extensions of Clause 2.3 are proposals. The route was validated (Clause 6).

=== How the example follows the diagram

==== Stereotypes and classes

|===
| Diagram element | Stereotype | In the JSON

| *Route* (`id`, `title`, `description`, `start`, `end`, `overview`, `actions`, `segments`, `attributes`)

| The *FeatureCollection*. `id`, `title`, `description` and *`actions`* (the route-level list) are members of the collection. `start`, `overview`, `segments` and `end` are its features. Constraint: `overview.duration` and `overview.length` equal the sums over the segments

| *RouteDefinition* (`start`, `end`, `mode`, `intermediateWaypoints`, `spatialConstraints`, `temporalConstraints`), composed in Route as role *`describedBy`*
| «property» attributes
| The *`describedBy`* member of the collection: the definition is part of the route response. It carries no actions in the diagram

| *RouteOverview* (`path`, `length`, `constraints`, `processingTime`, `comment`), `featureType = overview`
| «FeatureType»
| The `overview` feature. `path` (`GM_Curve`) is the `LineString` geometry; the other attributes are properties (4013 m, `duration_s` 469 s). It is not a `RouteWaypoint`, so it has *no actions*

| *Start* (`featureType = start`)
| «FeatureType»
| The `start` feature, a Point. It is a `RouteWaypoint`, so it has `actions`

| *RouteSegment* (`length`, `segment_actions`, `previousEnd`, `title`; `featureType = segment`)
| «FeatureType»
| One `segment` feature per leg, a Point at the *end* of the leg. `previousEnd` is the id of the feature where the leg starts. `segment_actions` apply along the leg

| *Destination* (`featureType = end`)
| «FeatureType»
| The `end` feature, a Point, with its `actions`

| *RouteWaypoint* (`actions`), a `Waypoint` (`title`, `constraint`, `id`, `location`)
| *no stereotype*
| *Not a feature type.* It is the supertype of `Start`, `RouteSegment` and `Destination`, so their features inherit `actions`, `title`, `constraint`, `id` and the Point `location`. The code-list value `route_waypoint` is not used by any feature type, so no feature has it

| *RouteComponent* (`featureType`, `attributes`)
| abstract
| `featureType` on every feature; `attributes` not used

| *Mode*, *SupportedRegion*, *SpatialConstraint*, *TemporalConstraint*
| «property» attributes
| Objects in `describedBy`. Enumerations `Domain` (`air`, `ground`, `maritime`), `RestrictionType` (`doNotEnter`, `doNotLeave`, `override`) and `TemporalConstraintType` (`noEarlierThan`, `noLaterThan`, `interval`) give the values

| *Action*
| none
| Our action instance (2.2)

| *Attribute*
| «Type»
| Not used
|===

==== The action instance

The `Action` class of the diagram has `id`, `title` and `description`. The examples keep the action definition used in the registries:

|===
| Member | Meaning

| `actionType`
| Identifier of a registry action (the registry holds its title, description and parameter schema)

| `target`
| System the action is addressed to (vehicle or payload)

| `sequence`
| Order among the actions of the same target, over the whole route (Clause 3)

| `params`
| Parameter values. Position parameters that are context parameters are filled from the point and omitted
|===

==== Extensions to the diagram

|===
| Where | Member | Meaning

| Route (collection)
| `actionRegistries`, `contingencies`, `targets`, `execution`, `vehicleClass`, `verticalReference`
| Registries used; `{condition, action}` pairs; Systems addressed; how a client executes; route options

| Overview
| `totalDuration_s`
| Duration including the time spent at waypoints (`duration_s` is the sum over the segments, as the constraint requires)

| Segment
| `travelMode`, `waypointId`, `timestamp`
| Means of travel on the leg; id of the definition's waypoint that the segment ends at (`Waypoint.id`); arrival time

| Start, end
| `timestamp`
| Time

| Segment
| (absent) `waypointId`
| A segment that the server *inserts* (a point the input did not name) has no `waypointId`
|===

=== Actions at three levels

|===
| Level | Model element | JSON | Issued | Reverted

| *Route*
| `Route.actions`
| `actions` on the feature collection
| When execution begins, in order, before the actions of the `start` feature
| Yes, after the actions of the `end` feature

| *Waypoint*
| `RouteWaypoint.actions`, inherited by `Start`, `RouteSegment` (its end point) and `Destination`
| `properties.actions` of `start`, `segment` or `end`
| On arrival at the feature's Point
| No

| *Segment*
| `RouteSegment.segment_actions`
| `properties.segment_actions` of a `segment`
| When the vehicle leaves the point named by `previousEnd`, so it applies along the leg
| Yes, at the segment's Point, before that point's own `actions`
|===

The order is: route actions; then, for each leg, the `segment_actions` of the segment, their reverts, and the `actions` at the segment's end point. The arrival actions of the *last* leg belong to the `end` feature and not to the last segment, because both have the same Point.

Which levels a command may appear at is part of its registry entry (`scope`). Route and segment levels are for settings and states that last: the executor undoes them when the level ends.

*Layering.* The route sets a cruise speed of 8 m/s (`vehicle.set-speed` at route level). Some segments set their own speed, and when such a segment ends the speed returns to 8 m/s. Segments without a speed action use the route-level speed.

=== The scenario

The multirotor `uav-07` starts from Pad A. At route level the executor powers the camera, selects the visible source, starts recording and sets the cruise speed. The vehicle takes off, flies a survey strip to tower T1 (photos every 20 m on the way, five zoomed photos at the tower), orbits tower T2 while recording video, hovers at a drop point and lowers a sensor on a winch, returns at a lower altitude and lands. The camera is switched off and recording stops by the route-level reverts. Travel time 7 min 49 s, 13 min 29 s with the time at waypoints, 4013 m, 9 features, 23 actions, 4 contingencies.

==== What the route server adds

|===
| Input | Output created by the server

| `start`
| The `start` feature, its time, and the actions at the start (take-off)

| `intermediateWaypoints` `tower-t1`, `tower-t2`, `sensor-site`
| One `segment` each (`waypointId` names the input waypoint), the planned altitude (3rd coordinate, MSL), the arrival time and the actions at the waypoint

| _(not in the input)_
| Two points the server inserted: `Ingress` and `Return corridor`, as segments without `waypointId`

| `end`
| The `end` feature and the landing

| `mode` (`uav-multirotor`, domain `air`)
| The allocated vehicle and payloads (`targets`), the speeds and the travel mode of the segments

| `spatialConstraints`
| A path that keeps inside the operating area and out of the no-fly zone (`overview.constraints` says so)

| `temporalConstraints`
| Times of the route that fit the daylight window

| _(nothing)_
| Route-level, segment-level and waypoint-level *actions*, the execution policy and the contingencies
|===

*Where the actions come from.* The server has what the input does not: the asset catalogue (tower T1 and T2 are inspection assets with a template: photos at T1, a video orbit at T2; the sensor site takes a winch drop), the capabilities of the allocated vehicle (camera, gimbal, winch), the operator's procedures (route-level recording and cruise speed, take-off and landing procedure), the registries of actions, and safety rules. The client only says _where_ and _within which limits_.

The route-level `sensor.set-power`, `camera.select-source`, `data.start-recording` and `vehicle.set-speed` are undone after the `end` feature's actions. `camera.set-trigger-distance` is undone at the end of segment `seg2`, and the speed of a segment goes back to the route-level speed.

Behaviours to notice:

* *Order across levels.* Route actions first; then for each leg the segment actions, then the actions at the end point. Each target's `sequence` increases along this order: the camera's route-level actions are 1--3, and its later actions follow.
* *Overriding the context position.* `gimbal.point-at` carries an explicit tower location. The vehicle position (the segment's Point) is a different place.
* *Paired actions.* `camera.start-video` / `camera.stop-video` and the winch `deploy` / `retract` surround the long-running `vehicle.orbit` and `payload.release`.
* *A latched setting at waypoint level stays.* `vehicle.set-vertical-position` in the `actions` of `seg5` is not reverted, so the lower altitude holds for the rest of the route.
* *Not in the route.* Arming and mission start have scope `none` and need explicit authorization.

=== Validation performed

For every action: the identifier exists in the registry; parameters exist and have the right type, range and enumeration; required parameters are present (context parameters excepted); the level (route, waypoint or segment) is in the action's `scope`; it applies to a UAV; `sequence` increases per target in the order of Clause 3. For the route: it is a feature collection; the only feature types are `overview`, `start`, `segment` and `end` (no `route_waypoint`), in the order overview, start, segments, end; `start` and `end` points equal the overview ends and the last segment ends at the destination; `overview.length` and `overview.duration_s` equal the sums over the segments; each segment's Point is on the overview path and its `previousEnd` refers to an earlier feature whose Point is on the path; the definition has only the members of `RouteDefinition`, and its waypoints only `id`, `title`, `location` and `constraint` (no actions); the segments that have a `waypointId` follow the input waypoints in order, with the same location and constraint; `describedBy` equals the input; `segment_actions` appear only on segments; the overview has no actions; timestamps increase. Contingency actions were checked for identifier and parameters.

=== Caveats

* Geometry between waypoints is a smooth interpolation, not a planned path.
* The definition has no actions: it is the input of the route server. Everything except the definition is the server's output.
* `timestamp`, `travelMode` and `waypointId` come from the Route Exchange Model or are additions, and are not attributes of the diagram's classes. `previousEnd` is used as the id of the feature where the leg starts.
* The diagram says `title` where earlier drafts of this series used `name`.
* Elevation is MSL; clients that need height above home or ground convert (see the MAVLink mapping).
